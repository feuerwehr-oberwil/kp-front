Digital incident command for the fire brigade.

KP Front is the digital command post at the scene: map, plans, teams, log and report in one app – on tablet, laptop and phone.

Grown out of the real operational work of Feuerwehr Oberwil BL – proven there, and developed further with every incident.

The measure

«Usable at 3am – after six months without practice»

In a militia fire brigade nobody sits at the screen every day. So the app has to be built for someone half asleep to get right: everything that matters visible at once, sensible defaults instead of settings, and every action can be undone.

The screenshots are in German: the public demo is, and they come from it – staged images would be a claim rather than evidence.

Situation map on paper

A situation picture on every device.

FKS tactical symbols, sectors, hose lines and notes on swisstopo maps. Hydrants, utility lines and vehicle positions sit underneath as layers. Take over the alert from Divera or FireHub with one tap.

  • Tactical symbols to FKS conventions – with floor, spread and notes
  • Water relay: pressure along the hose line and where the pumps go
  • Building info and immediate measures for the property

SCBA board and stopwatch

Monitoring that speaks up by itself.

Cylinder pressure, turnaround time and contact clock run per team. The app speaks up before it gets tight – even when nobody happens to be watching the clock. On the phone as on the tablet.

  • Contact clock and turnaround time per team
  • SCBA emergency on top at once, on every device
  • Teams with or without breathing apparatus, with their task

Building plan in a folder

A plan you can draw on.

Open the pre-incident plan, draw on it, place teams, measure to scale – with the same tools as on the map. The floor stack shows which team works on which storey.

  • Building and pre-incident plans of your own brigade
  • Georeferenced: what is on the map is on the plan too
  • Floor stack with the teams per storey

Scraps of paper and memory

A log that keeps up.

Type it or speak it. The log is only ever added to, never rewritten – corrections use new entries, not an eraser. Open tasks remind you by themselves.

  • Voice memos – as text with a transcription service
  • Open tasks with reminders
  • Capture sheet by QR code for everyone who contributes

Report the day after

The report as you head back.

Situation, crew, material and log flow straight into the report: check it, complete it, print it as a PDF. For the debrief, the replay runs the whole night once more.

  • Incident report as a finished PDF
  • The final check shows what is still missing
  • Replay: the incident minute by minute

Running it

Your data stays with you.

KP Front is open source. Every fire brigade runs its own installation – there is no central server, and nothing is sent to us automatically.

Free

AGPL-3.0: no licence fees, no subscription, no cost per device. What remains is your own cost for running it.

On your premises

On a mini PC in the station, your own server or a cloud provider of your choice. Works offline as an installable app.

No lock-in

All of the code is open: run it, adapt it, develop it further yourself.

Made to fit you

Your crew, vehicles, plans, checklists and map layers are set up in your own admin area – no programming needed.

Scope

What is in it.

Grown at real incidents and exercises, not from a feature list. Everything that comes from outside – paging, GPS, Microsoft sign-in – is optional: KP Front runs perfectly well without any of those connections.

Map & planTactical symbols · water relay · hazmat · building info · gas readings
  • Tactical symbols to FKS conventions, with attributes and labelling
  • Sectors, cordons, areas and freehand drawing
  • Hose lines with FKS annotations, each assigned to a team
  • Measuring distance and area, to scale on the plan as well
  • Water relay: pressure along a hose line from the height profile, hose diameter and flow – the calculation places the pumps itself, and can put them straight on the map
  • Layers: hydrants, utility cadastre, cantonal map services
  • Hazardous materials reference: UN numbers, substance information and guidance on isolation and protective distances, available directly during the incident
  • Weather and wind information to support the situation assessment, plus the MeteoSwiss precipitation radar as a layer
  • Building plans as a whiteboard and a stack of floors
  • Map & objects: outside an incident, your brigade’s own map – look up objects, hazards, hydrants and building plans
  • Board with FKS pages: free sketch, First command and handbook sheets – printed in the report like the original
  • Building info: facts from the building register – gas, heating oil, solar PV – and the immediate measures for the object
  • Readings: measuring points with gas readings per room and floor, with their history and alarm levels
  • Georeferencing: link a plan to the map through shared landmarks – one object, visible on both surfaces, with an honest read-out of the accuracy. The station has its plans aligned ahead of time and approves them
  • Undo/redo on every surface – documented events remain traceable
Crew & resourcesSCBA monitoring · attendance · shift plan · location sharing · material
  • SCBA monitoring: pressure, turnaround time, contact clock, alarms
  • SCBA emergency: a team in trouble turns red at the top of every device at once – with an emergency clock and the safety team
  • Teams with or without breathing apparatus: crew, assignment and team leader – on the map, on the plan and in the stack of floors, each with its own team colour, the same everywhere
  • Attendance with arrival times and ranks
  • On the way: who has confirmed the Divera alert, right above attendance
  • Shift plan: plan availability and shifts per person, see coverage across the incident and print command forms
  • Share location: whoever opens the link appears as a dot on the situation map – voluntary, and only for this incident
  • Share the incident by link; access is clearly indicated and can be revoked at any time
  • Presentation mode: everyone who has the incident open follows the presenter’s screen – all can point, nothing is stored
  • Material with stock and consumption
  • Checklists for command support and incident command
  • Incident commander role: keep attendance, resources, checklists and the report – read the map and the teams without moving anything
DocumentationLog · replay · report as PDF · station printer
  • A log that is only added to and never rewritten
  • Voice memos with transcription, photos, reminders
  • Replay: play the incident back minute by minute, waits skipped – for the debrief
  • Ready-made incident report as a PDF, including crew, material, times, log, sketch, photos and SCBA monitoring
  • Print the report and the shift plan straight to the station printer optional
  • Debrief sheet for the after-action review: key figures and a timeline on a page of its own at the end of the report
  • Photos as attachments to the report
  • Completion check: before closing, KP Front shows which information or clarifications are still missing from the report
  • Partner organisations with their remarks in the report

Questions & answers

The questions we get most.

Why switch?

Everyone sees the same situation at the same time – in the command vehicle, on the tablet and with the incident commander. No message is lost on a scrap of paper. The SCBA monitoring speaks up by itself before it gets tight. Less radio traffic for questions that are already answered on the situation picture – and the report is nearly done as you head back, instead of the weekend after. KP Front helps the incident commander keep the situation free of contradictions and make the odd one out visible: a team out of radio contact, an appliance that is nowhere, a check-in that never came.

Which fire brigades and incidents is KP Front for?

For militia, local, regional support, professional and industrial fire brigades – at fires, road traffic collisions, technical rescue, hazmat and oil spills, and major incidents. KP Front grew out of the operations of a militia fire brigade, so it is built for people who do not use it every day.

Where is sensitive incident data stored?

Every fire brigade runs KP Front itself (self-hosted). Incident data – situation map, SCBA monitoring, log and building plans – stays on its own installation and its own devices: for example a mini-PC at the station, its own server or a cloud provider it chooses. There is no central server, and nothing is sent automatically to a vendor or to us. Map, address and weather services (swisstopo among them by default) receive only the location being requested; other external services run only when the brigade sets them up itself.

How does KP Front differ from commercial systems?

KP Front is open source (AGPL-3.0): no licence fees, no subscription, no per-device fee. Every fire brigade runs its own installation and so keeps control of its data. What remains are its own costs for operation, infrastructure and any external support. Development comes straight from the practice of Feuerwehr Oberwil BL; bug reports, ideas and contributions from the open-source community are welcome.

Which devices does KP Front run on?

KP Front runs in the browser – no app store. It is built for the tablet: on an iPad and on Android tablets it installs to the home screen like an app and then also works without a connection. On a laptop in the command vehicle or at the station, the same interface runs with mouse and keyboard. The phone is the companion: look at the situation, add entries, keep an eye on the SCBA teams – drawing and commanding happen on the larger screen. All devices show the same incident.

What does it take to run?

A small mini-PC or server at the station is enough. Setup, updates and backups require someone with Docker and command-line experience, whether in the brigade, the municipality or an IT support provider. The guide and administration area lead through the setup. We are happy to answer general questions; data and operation remain the responsibility of each fire brigade.

Is support available for installation and operation?

Yes. General questions about installation and use can be answered free of charge. KP Front can be operated on an organisation’s own infrastructure or with a hosting or cloud provider of its choice. Support with installation, configuration, updates or operation is also available by individual agreement. More extensive support may be subject to a fee.

Does KP Front work without an internet connection?

Yes. KP Front can generally be used offline. Maps and other required data are stored locally. The app loads the maps and plans around an incident by itself, shortly after it is opened – and the readiness check shows before an incident whether the required data is on the device. Offline, weather and other time-dependent external data reflect the state of the last update. Several devices can be synchronised over an existing network connection, if needed also within your own local network without internet access. After an interruption, changes are synchronised again as soon as a connection is available; whatever was recorded in the meantime is kept.

Can we adapt it to how we work?

Yes. Every fire brigade has its own administration area: crew, vehicles, doctrine and templates are all set there. Checklists, building plans and map layers come from your own station data: you upload them one by one in the administration area, or many at once with the command-line tools that ship with it. And because the whole code is on GitHub , anything else can be built – by you or to your order.

Does KP Front only work in Switzerland?

No. As open-source software, KP Front is freely available and can be adapted to local conditions anywhere in the world. Out of the box it is preconfigured for Switzerland – with swisstopo maps, tactical symbols to FKS conventions and the FKS templates for incident command. Outside Switzerland other base maps can be configured; address search currently supports only the swisstopo interface and needs adapting there.

Which languages are available for KP Front?

The KP Front user interface comes in German, French, Italian and English. German is the authoritative version; the other three are complete but have not yet been proofread by firefighters. As an open-source application, KP Front can be extended to further languages. This requires a complete translation of the firefighting terminology and a professional review.

How do we start?

With the demo, no sign-up – Musterdorf is already on fire, and all visitors share the same incident (it resets twice a day, at 00:00 and 12:00 in the Europe/Zurich time zone). Then try it at an exercise. If it fits, the setup guide leads to your own installation.
