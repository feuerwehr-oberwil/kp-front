The measure

«Usable at 3am – after six months without practice»

In a militia fire brigade nobody sits at the screen every day. So the app has to be built for someone half asleep to get right: everything that matters visible at once, sensible defaults instead of settings, and every action can be undone.

From the call to the report

One incident, one app.

This is what a night with KP Front looks like – the situation, SCBA and documentation stay consistent and traceable for everyone involved.

03:01

Call

The call comes in.

Take over an alert from your paging system (e. g. Divera or FireHub) with one tap – or start by hand from an address, a building or the map. The location and the building data are there before the first appliance rolls.

03:07

Situation

The first situation picture is up.

FKS tactical symbols, sectors, hose lines and notes on the digital situation map. Hydrants, utility lines and vehicle positions sit underneath as layers.

03:12

SCBA

Team 1 goes in.

Cylinder pressure, turnaround time and contact clock run per team. The app speaks up before it gets tight – even when nobody happens to be watching the clock.

03:18

Plan

The building plan becomes a whiteboard.

Open the pre-incident plan, draw on it, place teams, measure to scale – with the same tools as on the map.

03:40

Log

Everything is in the log.

Type it, or simply speak it – when a transcription service is configured, voice memos can be turned into text; otherwise they stay as audio in the log. The log is only ever added to, never rewritten – corrections use new entries, not an eraser.

04:30

Shift plan

Relief is planned, not guessed.

If it turns into a long night, the shift plan holds, per person, from when to when they are available and when they are rostered. The coverage line shows where there are too few people – before the gap is there. Attendance is untouched by it: planning happens here, ticking off happens there.

05:55

Report

By the time you head back, the report is nearly done.

Situation, crew, resources and log flow straight into the incident report. Nobody has to reconstruct the night from memory afterwards.

Afterwards

Replay

The night runs once more.

For the debrief, the replay plays the whole incident back: situation, teams and log exactly as they stood at any given minute. It skips the long waits – four hours become a few minutes.

Scope

What is in it.

Grown at real incidents and exercises, not from a feature list. Everything that comes from outside – paging, GPS, printers – is optional: KP Front runs perfectly well without any of those connections.

Situation & plan

  • Tactical symbols to FKS conventions, with attributes and labelling
  • Sectors, cordons, areas and freehand drawing
  • Hose lines with FKS annotations, each assigned to a team
  • Measuring distance and area, to scale on the plan as well
  • Layers: hydrants, utility cadastre, cantonal map services
  • Building plans as a whiteboard, stack of floors, blank board
  • Undo/redo on every surface
  • Georeferencing: link a plan to the map through shared landmarks – symbols appear as twins on both surfaces, with an honest read-out of the accuracy

Crew & resources

  • SCBA monitoring: pressure, turnaround time, contact clock, alarms
  • Teams on the map, on the plan and in the stack of floors – each with its own team colour, the same everywhere
  • Attendance with arrival times and ranks
  • Shift plan: plan availability and shifts per person, see coverage across the incident and print command forms
  • Share location: whoever opens the link appears as a dot on the situation map – voluntary, and only for this incident
  • Share the incident by link; access is clearly indicated and can be revoked at any time
  • Resources and equipment with stock and consumption
  • Checklists for command support and incident command
  • Incident commander view: read along without moving anything

Documentation

  • A log that is only added to and never rewritten
  • Voice memos with transcription, photos, reminders
  • Replay: play the incident back minute by minute, waits skipped – for the debrief
  • Ready-made incident report as a PDF, including crew, resources, times, log, sketch, photos and SCBA monitoring
  • Photos as attachments to the report
  • Partner organisations with their remarks in the report
  • A capture sheet by QR code for everyone who has something to contribute
  • Alert and incident times from what actually happened, not from memory

Running it

  • Offline-capable as an installable app, readiness check included
  • Several devices at once, everything syncs by itself
  • Roles: edit, or read along only
  • Day/night mode, symbol size, large touch targets for the tablet
  • Can take over alerts from Divera or FireHub optional
  • Vehicle positions by GPS optional
  • PDF printing at the station optional
  • Your own administration area per fire brigade: crew, vehicles, checklists, map layers and templates
  • Interface in German, English, French or Italian – set per fire brigade

Questions & answers

The questions we get most.

Why switch?

Everyone sees the same situation at the same time – in the command vehicle, on the tablet and with the incident commander. No message is lost on a scrap of paper. The SCBA monitoring speaks up by itself before it gets tight. Less radio traffic for questions that are already answered on the situation picture – and the report is nearly done as you head back, instead of the weekend after. KP Front helps the incident commander keep the situation free of contradictions and make the odd one out visible: a team out of radio contact, an appliance that is nowhere, a check-in that never came.

Who owns the data?

You do. Maps, plans and incident data live on your own installation. Map and address services receive only the location being requested; other external services run only when you configure them. Nothing is sent automatically to a vendor or the maintainer.

What does it cost?

The software itself costs nothing. KP Front is open source (AGPL) – no licence, no subscription, no per-device fee. What remains are your own costs for operation, infrastructure and any external support you may need.

What does it take to run?

A small mini-PC or server at the station is enough. Setup, updates and backups require someone with Docker and command-line experience, whether in the brigade, the municipality or an IT support provider. The guide and administration area lead through the setup. We are happy to answer general questions; data and operation remain the responsibility of each fire brigade.

Can we adapt it to how we work?

Yes. Every fire brigade has its own administration area: crew, vehicles, doctrine and templates are all set there. Checklists, building plans and map layers come from your own station data and are loaded with the command-line tools that ship with it. And because the whole code is on GitHub , anything else can be built – by you or to your order.

What languages does the app run in?

The interface comes in German, English, French and Italian. The language is set once per fire brigade, not per device – during an incident everybody reads the same terms, and that is deliberate: one situation, one vocabulary. The public demo runs in German. The screenshots here are from that same instance – staged images would be a claim.

How do we start?

With the demo, no sign-up – Musterdorf is already on fire. Then try it at an exercise. If it fits, the setup guide leads to your own installation.
